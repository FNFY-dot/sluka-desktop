1. WHAT SLUKA DESKTOP DOES ON YOUR COMPUTER, AND WHAT IT SENDS
-------------------------------------------------------------

- Game text (the Game tab of the game text menu) puts LunaHook into
  the game you choose, to read its text.  Some games forbid that in
  their own terms -- online games, and games with anti-cheat software,
  above all -- and may ban an account for it.  Use it only with games
  that allow it.
- Sluka Desktop reads the screen or the clipboard only while you have
  the Screen tab, the Clipboard tab or Sluka Vision on, and keeps what
  it reads on your computer.
- Anki cards of a game's lines: while the floating subtitles have a
  game hooked, or Sluka Vision reads a game's window, Sluka Desktop
  keeps the last minute of that game's own sound -- its alone -- in
  memory, and lets it go with the game; when you add a word to Anki,
  it takes a picture of the game's window.  A word added in mpv takes
  the frame on screen (a file in the temporary folder, deleted once
  read) and reads its line's sound out of the film's file.  The clip
  and the picture go only onto that word's card, through AnkiConnect,
  into your own Anki (kept by Sluka until the card is made); nothing
  of them is sent anywhere else.
- The word panel's Look up key opens its dictionary sites in a small
  window of Sluka's own, whose web data (cookies and the like) are
  kept apart from the app's; each site's own terms apply to it.  A
  dictionary installed for offline use is downloaded when you ask for
  it, from its makers' own site, and kept under its own licence in the
  app's data folder: Wiktionary's entries from kaikki.org (CC BY-SA
  4.0 and GFDL), JMdict from the Electronic Dictionary Research and
  Development Group (CC BY-SA 4.0), CC-CEDICT from MDBG (CC BY-SA
  4.0), CC-Canto from Pleco Software (CC BY-SA 3.0) and Folkets
  lexikon from KTH (CC BY-SA 2.5).  None of them comes with Sluka
  Desktop.  The Translate key sends the word to Google Translate, as
  the extension's does, and the speaker, where no cloud voice is set
  up, asks Google Translate's voice to say it.
- Google Lens -- Sluka Vision's first reader, and one the Screen tab
  can be set to -- works online: each picture it reads is sent to
  Google, and Google's terms of service and privacy policy apply to
  it.  Google Lens is not a service that Google offers to other
  programs: Sluka reaches it the way Google's own Lens web page does,
  which Google has not agreed to, and Google may stop it at any time.
  Sluka asks you before Google Lens first reads; until you say yes,
  and if you say no, an offline reader reads instead.
- Sluka Desktop itself sends no statistics, and nothing else about you
  or about how you use it.  ONNX Runtime, the engine of Sluka's own
  OCR, is made by Microsoft to note how it is used, for Microsoft, in
  Windows' own diagnostic data; Sluka turns that off before its OCR
  starts.  ("sluka selftest", if you run it, looks whether a website,
  example.org, can be reached.)  The Sluka extension's own online
  features work as they do in a browser, under Sluka's terms.
